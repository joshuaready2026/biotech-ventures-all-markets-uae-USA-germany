BioTech Ventures Uses AI-Driven Design to Build Research Tools for the Central Nervous System

SCOTTSDALE, Ariz., November 17, 2026

BioTech Ventures is using AI-driven design to build laboratory research tools that help scientists study dysfunctions of the brain and central nervous system in ways today's psychedelics make hard. The trip can last for hours, often needs close supervision, and is difficult to study in an ordinary lab.

NeuroForge™ is a tunable modular platform of research tools for studying neuroplasticity and dysfunction in the brain and central nervous system, designed with the goal of separating plasticity from hallucinogenic activity. Proprietary, AI-driven computational software scores each starting point against public science before a dollar reaches the bench. A weak start is dropped. The software does not invent an atom and does not find a drug. They are for research use only, not a medicine. "Without the trip" is a design aim, not a result in people.

"If you want to study this biology in a regular lab, the trip gets in the way," said Josh Ready, founder and CEO of BioTech Ventures. "It can last for hours, someone has to stay and watch, and a lot of good questions never get asked. We use AI-driven design to build research tools for the central nervous system."

In support of that work, the company filed U.S. provisional patent application No. 64/131,190 (11 August 2026); company-owned; not an issued patent. The filing applies to substance use disorder, PTSD, and neurodegeneration, including Alzheimer's.

One core. Six programs. One attachment site. One method. The platform stays. Separate from the filing, four programs are live: substance use, PTSD, neurodegeneration, including Alzheimer's and ALS, and chronic pain. Autism and glioblastoma are later programs and have not started.
